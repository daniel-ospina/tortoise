# Research — Temporal QA Mechanics: Decomposition & Assembly for the Assembler

**Date:** 2026-09-08 · **Mode:** external evidence research (web; papers + systems + practitioner)
**Extends:** `docs/research/2026-09-02-temporal-reasoning-competitors.md` (vendor landscape, ranked read/write options). That doc covers Mem0 temporal metadata/state_key, Zep Graphiti bi-temporal edges, ChronoQA/TA-RAG/TempAgent one-liners. **This doc digs mechanics** on (a) query decomposition for temporal/comparative questions, (b) current-state resolution determinism, (c) relative-time + date-arithmetic mitigations, (d) what makes ordering/interval questions answerable, (e) what produced Mem0's multi-session +11.2. Findings only — no design.
**Context:** Tortoise LongMemEval-S temporal class (~133 q): ~43 ordering/compare, ~32 "ago"-relative, ~17 interval-between, + absolute-date/state/duration. Gold answers = dated events across sessions. Reader receives one assembled timeline.
**Searches:** Perplexity sonar (8 queries) + direct fetches of mem0 blog full text, ACL/arXiv pages. Rate-limited 3× mid-run; spacing applied.

## 1. Decomposition evidence (cost, when needed, deterministic alternatives)

### Decomposition improves temporal/comparative QA — with one strong quantified ablation
- **RTQA (arXiv:2509.03995, independent academic):** a *Temporal Question Decomposer* rewrites questions with implicit constraints — `"before"`, `"last"` — into explicit sub-questions, first resolving the implicit time. **Ablation removing decomposition drops Hits@1 overall 0.765 → 0.709, and the "Multiple" question type 0.424 → 0.214** (comparison/aggregation collapses without decomposition). **[HIGH]**. Caveat: the ablation removes the whole recursive pipeline, so the drop conflates decomposition + recursive retrieval — but the *category* that collapses is exactly the compare/aggregate class, consistent with the comparator evidence below.
- **RERC (Findings EMNLP 2021, aclanthology.org/2021.findings-emnlp.17):** explicit 3-stage pattern for comparative questions — **Relation Extractor decomposes → Reader answers sub-questions (one per side) → Comparator performs the numerical comparison** to produce the final answer. SOTA on 2WikiMultiHopQA (joint F1 53.58; 1.95 below human on support facts). This is the canonical architecture for "compare two things": per-side reads + a separate compare step, where the decomposition itself is the evidence path. **[HIGH]** (peer-reviewed, full abstract verified)
- **MultiTQ (ACL 2023, aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.637):** multi-granularity temporal questions (incl. comparisons spanning granularity boundaries) — now a standard eval for decomposition/rewriting methods. **[HIGH]** (peer-reviewed)
- **TEQUILA lineage (MPI-Inf QA page, mpi-inf.mpg.de/.../question-answering):** pre-LLM template-based decomposition: rewrite a temporal question into *non-temporal sub-questions + temporal constraints*, then combine sub-answers with **interval reasoning**. Shows the deterministic/template end of the decomposition spectrum — structure is detectable without an LLM. **[MEDIUM]**
- **Decomposed Prompting / DecomP-ODQA (arXiv:2210.02406, ICLR 2023, independent):** decomposer module + one sub-task handler per sub-question; large gains on multi-hop ODQA. **Cost structure is explicit: one LLM call per sub-task plus the decomposer call** (handlers can be cheap/stateless, and simple single-hop questions are not the target — decomposition is for complex tasks). **[HIGH]** (ID verified by fetch after a wrong-ID false start)

### When is decomposition actually the bottleneck? Mostly not — retrieval/assembly is
- **ChronoQA failure taxonomy (PMC full text of Nature Sci Data 2025, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12638886; nature.com/articles/s41597-025-06098-y):** 5,176 questions. **Retrieval failure = 72% of incorrect answers** (largest error source); **relative/implicit time expressions = 69% of analyzed failures**; **day-level granularity = hardest (62% of errors)**. Decomposition of the question is *not* the dominant failure class — getting the right dated evidence in hand is. **[HIGH]** (peer-reviewed; full-text verified)
- Cross-check from adjacent evals: **TimeBench (ACL 2024, aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.66)** reports a **25.2% gap vs humans on event-temporal reasoning**; a temporal-reasoning survey reports LLMs **up to 43% below human** on several datasets (openreview.net/pdf?id=jWBZdlU5Xl); temporal-robustness work shows **47–67% accuracy drops under date-format/reversal transformations** (Findings ACL 2025, aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.810); NAACL 2024 "Are LLMs Temporally Grounded?" finds **high inconsistency on mutually exclusive event orderings** (aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.391). Net: even with evidence present, reader LLMs mis-order and miscalculate — the reader is a real error surface, not just retrieval. **[HIGH]** on the gap numbers; the *specific* ordering-inconsistency claim is **[MEDIUM→HIGH]** (two papers).
- "Which came first" as a benchmark family exists and is KG-native: **TempQuestions (Freebase) → Wikidata adaptations (TimeQuestions / TempQA-WD, 664 test + 175 dev after removing KB-unanswerable; github.com/IBM/tempqa-wd; ibm.github.io/neuro-symbolic-ai/toolkit/tempqa-wd)** plus **CronQuestions / Complex-CronQuestions / MultiTQ** (see temporal-KGQA survey arXiv:2406.14191 and SABET-QA, arxiv.org/html/2608.20083). These encode ordering via entity-fact temporal qualifiers; answering requires both entities' dated qualifier facts (SPARQL shape). **[HIGH]** for existence/design; no controlled "LLM decomposition vs direct" head-to-head on these found — *absence noted*.

### Deterministic alternatives to LLM decomposition (evidence)
- **Entity-centric subgraph extraction instead of question decomposition:** arXiv:2402.13188 (Question Calibration & Multi-Hop Modeling) retrieves the *entity-centered subgraph* and combines sub-questions' information from it — i.e., when retrieval is organized around entities, per-side evidence arrives without splitting the question into LLM sub-queries. **[MEDIUM→HIGH]** (peer-reviewed)
- **Template/grammar detection:** the compare/order morphology ("which came first", "before/after", "earlier than") is closed-form relative to open decomposition — TEQUILA's constraint rewrite and TempQuestions' template families are deterministic structure. **[MEDIUM]** (lineage above)
- **Cost summary (sourced + inference):** LLM decomposition costs a decomposer call + a call+retrieval per sub-question (Decomposed Prompting architecture; RERC's reader-per-side); RTQA's temporal case additionally costs an implicit-time resolution pass. Deterministic alternatives trade that for (a) a template/type classifier (near-free) + (b) retrieval that returns *all* dated facts for both subjects in one pass. *Design-inference:* for ~43 ordering/compare questions the LLM-decomposition overhead is avoidable if the assembler recognizes the compare shape and fetches both subject timelines directly.

## 2. Current-state resolution patterns (deterministic vs LLM, per system)

### The split that matters: **LLM at write (typing + same-fact identity), deterministic at read (interval math)**
- **Mem0 (vendor; full mechanism verified by fetch of mem0.ai/blog/introducing-temporal-reasoning-in-mem0 + docs.mem0.ai/platform/features/temporal-reasoning):**
  - *Write:* a **separate temporal LLM pass** (explicitly "one additional model call per batch" in sync mode; async background patch in latency mode) assigns: when it happened, ongoing/completed, **time_precision** (day/week/month/year/approximate), and **memory type** (7: event/state/plan/relationship/preference/absence/timeless). Ongoing facts get a **state_key**; when a new state supersedes, "the old one's event_end gets set **automatically**" — the *closing* is a deterministic rule chained onto the LLM's state_key/type assignment. Nothing deleted. **[HIGH vendor]**
  - *Read:* query classified into temporal intent (**current_state, historical_range, duration_state, upcoming, soft_recency**…) **"with no extra LLM call"** (mechanism unspecified — likely classifier/heuristic; vendor does not disclose — *absence noted*). **Intent never pre-filters the candidate pool** — quoted rationale: "pre-filtering by time would silently drop memories with imprecise or missing dates"; instead **additive rerank** scoring stored time-metadata against intent; "semantic relevance always dominates." Low-precision dates are down-weighted, "represented, not silently dropped." **[HIGH vendor]**
  - Net determinism: current-state *selection* = deterministic scoring over LLM-written metadata (open state_key instance with no event_end wins for present-tense queries). The nondeterministic core is write-side: deciding *that* a memory is a state, *when* it started/ended, and *which older memory it supersedes* (state_key identity across sessions).
- **Zep/Graphiti (vendor docs + independent analyses):**
  - Edge validity windows: `valid_at`/`invalid_at` (docs variants `valid_from`/`valid_to`, `expired_at`). **"Active fact" = invalid_at is NULL**; "as of date t" = interval-containment filter; "now" = open windows (getzep.com/ai-agents/temporal-knowledge-graph/, vendor; deepwiki.com/getzep/zep/2.1-temporal-knowledge-graph, independent wiki; blog.getzep.com/beyond-static-knowledge-graphs, vendor). **[HIGH]**
  - An independent deep-dive (memoryatlas.dev/articles/zep-deep-dive) describes the invalidation *mechanic* as deterministic: "the old edge's validity window closes (an invalid_at is set), a new edge opens, nothing is deleted." **Tension flagged:** *what* contradicts/what supersedes is still an LLM decision upstream (extraction + fact resolution; prior 2026-09-02 doc already tagged this "LLM-judgment-dependent … wrong merges hard to undo"). So read-time "what's true now" is pure interval math, but the window *boundaries* are LLM-written. **[HIGH for read determinism; MEDIUM for the write-side determinism claim — memoryatlas overstates vs vendor docs]**
- **Bitemporal provenance (both systems + prior doc):** assertion-time validity (valid_from/to) and ingestion time (observed/recorded/capturedAt) kept separate — "as of" queries operate on assertion time without erasing provenance. Tortoise's `capturedAt` + `startedAt/endedAt` map 1:1. **[HIGH]**
- **What is NOT deterministic anywhere found:** entity resolution and "same evolving fact" identity (Mem0 state_key assignment; Graphiti merge/invalidate decisions) are LLM judgments at write in both production systems. No evidence of a system doing this deterministically. **[HIGH]** — this is the load-bearing nondeterminism, and it is a *write-side* cost both vendors paid.

## 3. Relative-time + date-arithmetic mitigations with evidence

### Relative/"ago" expressions: anchoring is the practice; arithmetic must be taken from the reader
- **Reference-date injection is documented practice:** TA-ARE (Findings ACL 2024, aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.415) explicitly prompts **"Today is current_date()"** to make time-sensitive adaptive retrieval work — direct support for Tortoise's existing "Current Date:" + per-session-date headers. **[HIGH]**
- **ChronoQA:** implicit/relative expressions = 69% of failures; the dataset breakdown is 3,176 implicit vs 2,000 explicit time expressions (PMC full text + table). Relative time is the hard class by construction. **[HIGH]**
- **TA-RAG (arXiv:2507.22917):** temporal parsing at query time + event-interval-based retrieval; on diachronic QA, disabling time filtering measurably degrades (ADQAB benchmark). Relevant to "ago" → the *parsed interval* drives retrieval, not the raw words. **[HIGH]**
- **Mem0 (vendor):** relative expressions are resolved **at write time against the conversation/session date** — the temporal pass reads "the memory + conversation + the date it happened" and stores the resulting date. i.e., anchors are per-session, not global; the reader never sees "two weeks ago" unresolved. **[HIGH vendor]**
- **Practitioner consensus on resolving "this Friday / two weeks ago" (multi-source, anecdote tier [MEDIUM]):** frontier models still produce off-by-one weekday-relative dates — recommendations converge on: LLM extracts the reference + the offset, **deterministic `resolve_date`/date lib does the arithmetic**, server supplies the current date (reddit.com/r/OpenAI/comments/1uwb7pe; stackoverflow.com/questions/78347436; dev.to/maverickyadav/-llms-cant-reliably-do-date-math-4hm2; community.openai.com/t/how-to-correctly-process-time-references-like-tomorrow/577757; lune.dev). Not controlled evals — practitioner signal, but convergent across independent sources and consistent with DateLogicQA below.
- **Date-arithmetic unreliability (controlled):** **DateLogicQA (NAACL 2025 SRW, aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-srw.32; arXiv:2412.13377):** 190 human-curated date-format/arithmetic questions; documents **representation-level and logical-level bias**, and ties tokenization (date fragmentation; Semantic Integrity Metric) to errors on historical/futuristic dates. **[HIGH]** (peer-reviewed)
- **Day-level granularity is the hardest band in RAG too:** ChronoQA day-level reasoning = 62% of errors (see §1). "How many days between A and B" lives exactly in the hardest measured granularity. **[HIGH]**
- **Mitigation evidence stack:** (1) anchor injection (TA-ARE), (2) resolve relative→absolute at write/assembly against session/current date (Mem0 pattern; practitioner `resolve_date`), (3) perform diffs/ordering with deterministic date code, not the reader (practitioner convergence; DateLogicQA), (4) represent imprecision explicitly so it down-weights rather than fabricates (Mem0 `time_precision` [HIGH vendor]).

## 4. What makes ordering/interval questions answerable (the assembly contract for the reader)

Synthesized from the evidence above (each clause maps to a cited finding):

1. **Both halves must be in the pool.** Ordering/compare questions fail at retrieval before any reasoning (ChronoQA 72% retrieval-failure share [HIGH]); compare-specific evidence is the RTQA "Multiple"-type collapse (0.424→0.214) when the machinery that fetches per-side facts is removed [HIGH]. "Which came first — X or Y" needs X's dated event **and** Y's — one-sided retrieval cannot be rescued by any reader.
2. **Dated facts, not prose, are the unit of assembly.** KG-native ordering benchmarks (TempQuestions→TempQA-WD, CronQuestions, MultiTQ) answer via temporal qualifiers on entity facts [HIGH]; interval arithmetic is well-defined over dates, undefined over recalled prose.
3. **Comparison should be a separate step from reading each side** (RERC: reader-per-side → comparator) [HIGH]. A single reader asked to both recall and compare compounds the inconsistency measured on mutually exclusive orderings (NAACL 2024 temporally-grounded; robustness reversal drops 47–67%) [MEDIUM→HIGH].
4. **Ordering/interval math should be precomputed or checked deterministically.** LLM day-count/ordering errors are documented (DateLogicQA [HIGH]; day-level hardest in ChronoQA [HIGH]; practitioner off-by-one convergence [MEDIUM]). Handing the reader "A(2024-03-14) before B(2024-11-02); diff = 233d" makes the residual a *given-fact comprehension* task, not arithmetic.
5. **Relative expressions should arrive resolved to anchors, or with an explicit anchor + offset to resolve deterministically** — never as bare "ago" text (TA-ARE anchor injection [HIGH]; Mem0 write-time resolution against session date [HIGH vendor]; practitioner resolve_date [MEDIUM]).
6. **For state/"most recent" the current instance is the one with no end marker** — deterministic open-window/`event_end`-null selection on LLM-written write-time metadata [HIGH vendor/independent: Mem0 event_end; Graphiti invalid_at NULL] — and history must survive for the "what was true before" half of comparisons (no deletion in either system [HIGH]).
7. **What the reader alone still decides** (no deterministic substitute found): judging *sameness* of entities/facts across sessions (state_key identity, entity resolution — LLM at write in both Mem0 and Graphiti [HIGH]) and, at read, any reasoning left to the model beyond comparing presented dates (a bounded, evidence-present surface given items 2–5).

## 5. Implications for Tortoise's assembler (labeled; findings-only framing)

1. **Decomposition cost is avoidable for the ~43 ordering/compare + ~17 interval questions** — the compare shape is template-detectable and the deterministic alternative (fetch both subjects' dated event timelines in one entity-centric pass, then sort/diff) matches the strongest quantified failure and gain evidence: ChronoQA's 72% retrieval-failure dominance [HIGH] and RTQA's Multiple-type collapse when per-side assembly is removed [HIGH]. LLM query decomposition (decomposer + per-sub-question calls, Decomposed Prompting/RERC) buys the same thing at N+1 LLM calls; the deterministic route obtains it from entity-centric retrieval (arXiv:2402.13188 pattern). *Where LLM decomposition still earns its calls:* implicit-time resolution ("last Saturday" → date) — RTQA shows that pass is the valuable one — which for Tortoise maps to the relative-time intent class (~32 questions), where ChronoQA's 69%-of-failures class lives [HIGH].
2. **Current-state resolution is already deterministic-in-principle on Tortoise's schema** — open lifecycle (`no endedAt`), open validity (`valid_to` NULL), non-superseded (no CORRECTS/superseded_by successor) is exactly Mem0's event_end-null and Graphiti's invalid_at-NULL "active fact" selection [HIGH vendor + independent]. Both production systems put their only nondeterminism on the write side (state/entity identity), which Tortoise mirrors (LLM-dated events + Object lifecycle). The assembler's "latest state" pass is interval arithmetic, not LLM judgment — provided write-side supersession edges/end markers are populated.
3. **Relative-time handling is Tortoise's best-aligned evidence area**: Tortoise already passes "Current Date:" + session dates (TA-ARE's documented practice [HIGH]); the gap is resolving "ago" to concrete dates at assembly time and *never* handing the reader bare relative expressions or arithmetic duties (Mem0 resolves at write against session date [HIGH vendor]; DateLogicQA + practitioner evidence says arithmetic stays out of the reader [HIGH/MEDIUM]).
4. **Multi-session gains came from both-halves availability, not from intent magic**: Mem0's own mechanism story is (a) state_key keeps every dated instance of an evolving fact retrievable, (b) deep pools (top_50) contain both instances, (c) additive temporal rerank picks the intent-matching one while deletion-free history preserves the other half [HIGH vendor]. Direct implication: **entity-centric event assembly is the deterministic equivalent of "state_key co-retrieval"** — both halves are guaranteed by construction instead of by semantic similarity + pool depth. For ordering/compare, "most recent" reranking is the wrong axis entirely; the assembler needs both instances regardless of intent.
5. **Reader contract bound by measurement, not hope**: even well-evidenced readers mis-order and miscalculate (25.2% TimeBench gap [HIGH]; ordering inconsistency [MEDIUM→HIGH]; day-level 62% [HIGH]). The assembly contract implied by the evidence: deliver *sorted, ISO-dated, pre-diffed* event lists and state snapshots (both halves present, end-markers explicit), so the reader's residual job is comprehension over given dated facts — the configuration where every cited benchmark still shows *some* reader error but where the error surface is bounded and attributable.
6. **Absence-of-evidence log:** (a) no independent, mechanism-level replication of Mem0's +11.2 — vendor-only, and the blog's own headline numbers are internally inconsistent ("94.8% at top_50 up from 94.4%" vs text "90.4 → 94.4 at top_200"); treat the *mechanism* as credible (consistent with the design of their system and with ChronoQA's retrieval-failure finding) but the magnitude as unverified. (b) No controlled head-to-head found for "LLM decomposition vs entity-centric assembly" specifically over dated event graphs — closest are RTQA ablation (KG-QA) and ChronoQA failure taxonomy (RAG). (c) No evidence found that any system resolves relative dates deterministically at read time — all resolve at write/assembly with LLM-extracted anchors; Tortoise passing anchors to the reader is the practice, with deterministic arithmetic as the recommended addition. (d) "No extra LLM call" for Mem0's temporal-intent classification is undisclosed in mechanism — treat as unverified.
