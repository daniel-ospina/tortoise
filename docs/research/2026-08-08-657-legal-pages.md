# Research Brief — Legal Pages for tortoise.premiselabs.co (#657)

## 1. Problem reframing

Tortoise needs public legal pages that do three jobs at once: **(a) build trust** with technical users who evaluate agent-memory products against Mem0/Zep and will read the ToS/Privacy page before connecting an API key; **(b) be GDPR/CCPA/analytics-ready** so the pages don't need rewriting when PostHog/GA/Meta Pixel instrumentation ships (#528); and **(c) draw a crisp boundary between the BUSL-1.1 self-hosted product and the hosted SaaS** — including Tortoise's stricter-than-market privacy stance (no AI training on user data, no data sale) which is a genuine differentiator vs Mem0 and Letta. The pages must be template-based, cheap, and deployable as static HTML on Cloudflare Pages today.

## 2. What we have internally (verified this session — High)

- **License:** BUSL-1.1 (`.worktrees/657-legal/LICENSE`). Parameters: Licensor "Premise Labs (Daniel Ospina)"; free self-hosted production use for orgs ≤ US $5M trailing-12-month revenue; above threshold → commercial license; grant never permits offering Tortoise as a hosted/managed service to third parties; hosted service explicitly excluded from the grant ("licensed separately as a commercial product, free tier available"); Change Date 4 years → **MPL 2.0** (GPL-2.0-compatible, keeps canonical BSL text intact).
- **License rationale** (`docs/license-notes.md`, High): BSL + revenue threshold protects the self-host/trust segment while enabling monetization; MPL 2.0 conversion is the "enterprise-safe" choice (HashiCorp precedent); single copyright holder (Daniel Ospina, 536 commits, no CLA risk). FAQ answers for "when do I need a commercial license" are drafted there.
- **Site topology** (`website/functions/_middleware.ts`, High): only `/` is rewritten (→ `product.html` for `tortoise.*` hosts); every other path serves its own asset. So `privacy.html` + `tos.html` will automatically be served at `/privacy` and `/tos`. Existing pages: `product.html`, `welcome.html`, `signin.html`, `signup.html`, `self-hosted.html`, `docs.html`, `index.html`.
- **Existing legal bits:** `docs/legal/connor-ip-agreement.md` (IP assignment); `legal/index.md` = empty domain slot; `docs/license-notes.md` FAQ. No company email exists — LICENSE contact line points to GitHub issues.
- **Data practices to enshrine** (product owner): collect email/login (account + billing), usage data (product improvement), analytics = Meta Pixel + GA + PostHog; no PII beyond that; no data sale; no AI training on user data. **No analytics code is deployed yet** (PostHog gated by #528).

## 3. External findings

### 3.1 Template landscape

**Common Paper (commonpaper.com) — the best fit, free and open-source (CC BY 4.0) — High.** All standard agreements are drafted by a committee of dozens of attorneys, free to use/modify under Creative Commons CC BY 4.0, downloaded 40,000+ times. Two-part structure: **Cover Page** (variables: Order Form + Key Terms) + **Standard Terms** hosted online and incorporated by reference; or download the full combined document.

- **Cloud Service Agreement (CSA) v2.1** (released Nov 5, 2024; current) — the template Zep's terms are built on. Section list (fetched primary text): §1 Service Access and Use / Support / User Accounts / Feedback and Usage Data / Customer Content / **Machine Learning**; §2 Restrictions & Obligations (Restrictions on Customer incl. no reverse engineering, no competing-product development; Suspension); §3 Privacy & Security (Personal Data → **DPA required before GDPR personal data is submitted**; Prohibited Data); §4 Payment & Taxes; §5 Term & Termination (incl. Force Majeure, Effect of Termination, **Survival**); §6 Representations & Warranties; §7 Disclaimer of Warranties; §8 Limitation of Liability; §9 Indemnification; §10 Confidentiality; §11 Reservation of Rights; §12 General Terms; §13 Definitions.
  - ⚠️ **Critical:** the v2.1 **Machine Learning clause** defaults to allowing the provider to use aggregated, de-identified Usage Data **and Customer Content to train AI/ML models** (including third-party components). Tortoise's no-training stance requires **deleting/negating this clause** — it is the single most important template customization. (Zep uses CSA **v1.1**, which predates the ML clause — Zep's terms are silent on training.)
  - v2 vs v1: v2 adds trials/pilots support, flexible payment terms, and the AI-training language.
- **Terms of Service template** (same standard terms, clickthrough format) — "a customized version of the CSA… structured to be posted online by a vendor and for customers to click-to-accept." Workflow: fill the Cover Page variables → post at `/tos` → link on signup form; standard terms incorporated by reference; a signed CSA Cover Page controls over the clickthrough ToS (upsell path stays consistent). **This is the exact pattern Tortoise needs.**
- **Other free templates in the same suite (all CC BY 4.0):** **Data Processing Agreement (DPA)** — directly reusable, zero cost; **SLA**; **AI Addendum** (covers AI data-use/IP/risk — relevant for the no-training stance); **Software License Agreement** (on-prem/self-hosted); **NDA**; Order Form; PSA; BAA. Costs: $0 (CC BY 4.0 attribution required); no login needed to download.

**Generator services (for reference — higher cost, hosted, not recommended as primary base) — Medium:**
- **iubenda**: free basic plan; paid from ~€4.99/mo; EU-focused (GDPR/ePrivacy), legal-expert maintained.
- **Termly**: free plan = 1 policy + banner; paid from ~$10/mo.
- **TermsFeed**: one-time fee per generated document + clause add-ons; consent-management billed monthly.
- All three output policy text/banners that embed as static HTML/JS — workable on Cloudflare Pages but subscription-based and generic; Common Paper is a better base for a B2B dev-tool ToS because it's structured for SaaS/clickthrough specifically.
- **ToS;DR (tosdr.org)**: peer-reviewed A–E grading of companies' terms — useful as an *evaluation* resource (post-launch check), not a template; lags updates and covers only major services (Low confidence on coverage claims — single source).

### 3.3 Analytics / consent / DPA requirements

**Meta Pixel — High:** GDPR requires explicit opt-in consent (ePrivacy applies; consent must be "freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous"); the privacy policy must name the tool and disclose controller, purposes, legal basis, recipients, retention (Meta dev docs + practitioner guidance). Joint-controller theory (Fashion ID) can pull the site operator into Art. 82 liability. **Enforcement is real:** German OLG Dresden awarded €1,500/user damages over Meta Business Tools; a Swedish online pharmacy was fined 8M SEK for unlawful Meta Pixel data transfer; Austrian DPA found Meta Pixel in violation. Under **CCPA/CPRA**, sharing personal info with Meta for cross-context behavioral advertising may count as "sale" **or** "sharing" → separate disclosure + "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link.

**Google Analytics 4 — Medium/High:** Google relies on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework (+ UK/Swiss extensions) and SCCs where required; the **Google Ads Data Processing Terms** must be accepted (in Analytics settings) for EEA-GDPR organizations; GA4 reportedly doesn't log/store EU-user IPs. Practitioner caution (Usercentrics): DPF alone does not make GA4 compliant — consent + disclosure still needed.

**PostHog — High:** PostHog Cloud acts as **data processor** (customer = controller); **PostHog Cloud EU is hosted in Frankfurt** (EU data residency option); PostHog certifies to EU–US DPF, UK Extension, Swiss–US DPF and uses SCCs for EEA/UK/CH transfers; their privacy docs include GDPR-consent configuration and right-to-be-forgotten guidance; EU orgs default to IP capture disabled.

**DPA timing — High:** Under GDPR Art. 28(3), a DPA is **mandatory before processing begins** whenever a processor relationship exists — "the trigger is the controller-processor relationship, not the size of the deal: a free-tier vendor that touches personal data still needs a DPA." So the DPA question is *now-or-at-first-business-customer*, not optional. Common Paper's DPA template is free.

**Consent banner for a STATIC Cloudflare Pages site — Medium/High:** No backend is needed — pure JS banners work. **Klaro** (open source, free forever, static-site suitable, implicit+explicit consent, blocks third-party apps until consent) is the best open-source fit; PostHog's own JS SDK includes consent tooling (natural if PostHog ships via #528); Cookiebot/Osano are commercial hosted CMPs (heavier, subscription — overkill at this stage). Since no analytics is deployed yet, the *pages* must disclose the tools + consent mechanism now; the banner itself only activates when instrumentation ships.

### 3.4 Governing law / jurisdiction conventions — Medium

Typical candidates for US SaaS: **California, Delaware, or New York** (terms.law); Common Paper's own clause library names Delaware and California as common choices; jurisdiction is often the state where the business is based (TermsFeed guidance). **Zep chose Delaware** for both law and courts. YC-stage companies are usually Delaware-incorporated, and matching governing law to the entity's incorporation state is the convention. If the entity is Mexican, **LFPDPPP** applies: a privacy notice (Aviso de Privacidad — comprehensive + simplified formats) is required before collection, with ARCO rights — separate from, and additional to, GDPR compliance for EU users. **Entity jurisdiction of Premise Labs is UNKNOWN — this is the key open decision; the pages' controller-identity + governing-law clauses cannot be finalized without it.**

### 3.5 Adversarial findings (disconfirmation)

- **Revenue-threshold ambiguity is a known BSL failure mode — High (Directus, dotCMS).** Directus left BSL partly because "gray areas" (what counts as finances, subsidiaries/parents, production use) created enforcement inconsistency; dotCMS responded by defining "Total Finances" extremely broadly (revenue, budget, or funding). Tortoise's AUG says "total annual revenue of your organization" — subsidiary/group accounting is genuinely ambiguous. The commercial-license/FAQ surface should acknowledge the threshold question; the ToS should not contradict the LICENSE.
- **Copy-paste ToS risks — High (multiple practitioner sources).** Copying another company's terms imports promises never intended (e.g., Mem0's free-plan training clause), product-specific gaps, outdated or wrong-jurisdiction language; recurring traps are weak/missing liability caps, uncapped indemnities, auto-renewal ambiguity, and unclear clickthrough acceptance. Mitigation: start from Common Paper (built for SaaS, current), customize the variables, delete the ML clause, and review the cap.
- **BSL is source-available, not OSI-approved — High (license-notes + OpenTechHub).** Distro inclusion is blocked during the restricted period and some enterprise allowlists exclude it (same class as AGPL/SSPL). Not a blocker for the legal pages, but the ToS should not describe Tortoise as "open source."
- **Meta Pixel risk is asymmetric for small sites — Medium/High.** Fines have hit operators (8M SEK pharmacy) but most German damages awards so far targeted Meta; exposure is real but manageable with consent + disclosure + (if desired) EU-resident hosting.

## 4. Recommendations (synthesis)

1. **Base ToS = Common Paper CSA v2.1 clickthrough variant** (free, CC BY 4.0, the same foundation Zep uses). Post the completed Cover Page at `/tos`; keep Standard Terms incorporated by reference (optionally mirror the text locally for control). Customizations:
   - **Delete/negate the Machine Learning clause** → replace with explicit "we do not use your content or usage data to train AI models" (aligns with product owner mandate; differentiator vs Mem0/Letta).
   - Add a **License boundary section**: self-hosted use is governed by BUSL-1.1 (link to GitHub LICENSE); the hosted service is a separate commercial product, free tier available; revenue threshold → commercial license contact (LICENSE already points to GitHub issues).
   - Keep CSA's DPA trigger (§3), liability cap (recommend "fees paid in prior 12 months" — Zep convention; cap free tier at a flat amount or same rule), no-benchmarking restriction, termination/survival defaults.
2. **Privacy Policy sections** (static `privacy.html`): controller identity (+ GDPR Art. 27 rep if an EU presence exists); data categories (account/email/login, usage data, analytics via Meta Pixel/GA4/PostHog); purposes; cookies + consent (ePrivacy); sharing/processors (PostHog Cloud EU Frankfurt, GA4 + Google Ads Data Processing Terms, Meta joint-controller disclosure); international transfers (DPF + SCCs); retention; user rights (GDPR + CCPA/CPRA — statutory **no-sale** wording AND separate **"sharing" disclosure** for Meta Pixel + "Do Not Sell or Share" link); **explicit no-AI-training pledge**; contact.
3. **Footer link set:** Privacy Policy · Terms of Service · License (→ GitHub LICENSE; a `/license` page explaining BUSL + $5M threshold + MPL 2.0 conversion is standard practice — HashiCorp, Sentry, MariaDB all expose license/BSL pages) · Security (later) · DPA (later). A dedicated `/legal` hub page (HashiCorp/Sentry/Supabase pattern) is optional now, trivial to add.
4. **DPA: ship now, publicly** — cost ≈ $0 (Common Paper DPA template, CC BY 4.0), required by Art. 28(3) before any processor relationship exists (even free tier), and it's the trust signal enterprise buyers look for (Supabase/Sentry precedent). Minimum fallback: keep CSA §3's DPA-before-personal-data clause and defer the standalone page to first business customer.
5. **Consent banner:** not needed until #528 ships; plan **Klaro** (open-source, static-compatible) or PostHog's consent tooling. State in the privacy policy now that consent is obtained for non-essential cookies/pixels.
6. **Governing law:** default recommendation — **match to the entity's incorporation state once confirmed** (Delaware if DE-incorporated — YC convention, Zep precedent; California if CA entity; if Mexican entity, add LFPDPPP Aviso de Privacidad). Do not hardcode until the entity question is answered.
7. **Do NOT describe Tortoise as "open source"** in legal copy — BSL is source-available ("eventually" open source on Change Date).
