## 0. Answer in one line

Of the 10 residual ON misses: **1 is NOT REACHABLE (A)**, **9 are REACHABLE-BUT-DROPPED (B)**,
**0 are REACHED-BUT-WRONG-READER (C)**. The guard is a pure *per-session ceiling of 2 on the
top-5 ranks* with **no term for an unrepresented session below the window** — so it can only
move a question whose OFF top-5 is **monopolised** (one session ≥3 of the 5), and even then it
admits the next *rank-ordered* session, which is label-blind and need not be the gold one.

## 1. The guard's exact structural condition

`session_diverse_order` — `tortoise/coverage_loop.py:473-509`, condition at **line 499**:

```python
for h in items:
    s = _session_of(h, session_key)
    if len(windowed) >= window or counts.get(s, 0) >= per_session_cap:
        deferred.append(h)
    else:
        windowed.append(h)
        counts[s] = counts.get(s, 0) + 1
...
return windowed + deferred
```

* window = **5** — `tortoise/coverage_loop.py:68` (`DEFAULT_LOOP_GUARD_WINDOW`), aliased
  `tortoise/retrieval.py:50` as `DEFAULT_POOL_GUARD_WINDOW`.
* per_session_cap = **2** — `tortoise/coverage_loop.py:73`, aliased `tortoise/retrieval.py:53`.
* reachable through the shared contract `guard_and_recap_pool` — `tortoise/retrieval.py:662`
  (guard → C5 re-cap); the injection path calls it from
  `tortoise/session_reinjection.py:469-472` (`reinjection_merge_order`), which the eval invokes at
  `tools/longmem_eval/retrieve.py:1823-1828` with `guard_window=_LOOP_WINDOW` (=5).

**The lesson is the two invariants that follow from those three lines:**

1. **The condition is a CEILING, never a FLOOR.** It defers the 3rd+ row of a session; it never
   reserves a slot for a session that is absent from the window. Once the 5 slots are held by
   rows of 3+ distinct sessions (each ≤2), the predicate is unsatisfiable and the guard is a
   **provable identity** on the top-5.
2. **The `window` knob is inert with respect to the graded metric.** The result is
   `windowed + deferred`, so the top-5 is `windowed[:5]`, which is invariant to `window` for any
   `window ≥ 5`. Verified: the top-5 is **identical for window ∈ {5,10,40,200} on 71/71
   questions**. The only knob that can change the graded top-5 is `per_session_cap`.

**Class of miss the guard can reach:** only where the OFF top-5 is monopolised by one session
(≥3 of the 5), *and* the earliest rank-ordered session admitted in place of the surplus rows
happens to be gold. **Class it cannot reach:** (i) any OFF top-5 already holding ≥3 distinct
sessions (guard = identity), (ii) any gold session deeper than the window's fill point
(≈ 5 + deferrals).

### Reach census over the 23 OFF misses (monopoly is a perfect discriminator)

| OFF top-5 | n | closed by ON | residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| **monopolised** (one session ≥3 of the 5) | 19 | **13** | 6 |
| **not monopolised** | 4 | **0** | **4** |

The 4 non-monopolised misses are exactly the 4 where `deferrals == 0` (the guard fills 5 slots
without deferring anything) and the guard's top-5 is byte-identical to the OFF top-5:
`078150f1`, `ba358f49`, `ba358f49_abs`, `d905b33f`.

The confirmation from `inj_only` (0/0 on `session_recall@5`) is structural: injection splices each
seeded session's group **immediately after that session's LAST base rank**
(`tortoise/session_reinjection.py:441-443` computes `last_rank`; `:458-463` splices), and in the
residual set the injected rows land at ON ranks far below the top-5 (min 26, typically 55-99, and
as deep as 126 on `ba358f49`, §2) — never in or near the top-5. Re-injection alone adds material
deep in the pool; only the guard's reorder can lift anything, and only from the monopolised class.

## 3. The cap is the only metric-moving knob, and it is a blunt lever

Because `window` is inert (§1.2), the only guard parameter that can fix (B) is
`per_session_cap`.

**Method (cheap, offline).** The C4 merge is reconstructible exactly:
base = recorded OFF `ranked_ids`; injected rows = ON-minus-OFF, grouped by their session and kept
in their recorded relative order; each group spliced after its session's last base rank in seed
order (the rule at `session_reinjection.py:441-469`). Replaying the **shipped** guard
(`cap=2`) on that reconstruction reproduces the recorded ON `ranked_ids` **on 71/71 questions,
full order — not just the top-5.** The reconstruction is therefore faithful, so a `cap=1` replay
on it is an exact projection of the ordering effect.

**Projection at `per_session_cap = 1` (window=5):**

* closes **5 of the 9 (B)** — `078150f1`, `8979f9ec`, `ba358f49_abs`, `c18a7dc8`, `cc06de0d`;
* residual falls **10 → 5** (`ba358f49` = (A), plus `8e91e7d9`, `a1cc6108`, `bc149d6b`,
  `d905b33f`, each of which has >5 distinct sessions ranked ahead of its gold session);
* `recall_all@5` projection: **0.8592 → 0.9296** (61 + 5) / 71;
* all 13 already-closed questions are preserved, and distinct-gold coverage is monotone
  (`cap=1 ⊇ cap=2` on 71/71 — no question loses a gold *session*).

**The cost, named honestly:** `cap=1` forces 5 distinct sessions into a 5-slot window, which by
construction ejects each gold session's 2nd row. On this reconstruction it puts **one fewer gold
evidence item into the top-5 on 20 of 71 questions** (e.g. `09ba9854`, `3fe836c9`, `4adc0475`,
`ef66a6e5` go 2→1; `d905b33f` goes 1→0). That trade is invisible to `recall_all@5` and visible to
`evidence_recall@5` — and the shipped ON arm already regresses `evidence_recall@5` on 11
questions. So `cap=1` is a real decision, not a free fix.
