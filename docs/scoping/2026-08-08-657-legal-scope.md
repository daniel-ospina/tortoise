## Phase 5 — Solution converge

### 5.1 Decision: **Approach A** — self-contained static HTML, Common Paper CSA v2.1 as the *drafting base*, cover-page variable discipline, all text mirrored locally.

Rationale (quality over convenience):
1. **Legal pages are compliance artifacts — immutability wins.** Incorporation-by-reference (B) means Common Paper's next edit to v2.1 silently rewrites our ToS. A static site has no backend to pin/redirect a stale reference. A self-contained mirror keeps the exact text under git history, reviewer-diffable, and stable at the URL.
2. **CC BY 4.0 explicitly permits** copying/adapting Common Paper templates (attribution line required — kept in the `tos.html` footer). We pay $0 and keep full control.
3. **The customizations are the point** — negating the v2.1 Machine Learning clause (which defaults to *allowing* training on Usage Data + Customer Content — the exact opposite of D5) and adding the BSL boundary section. Approach A makes those edits first-class, reviewable diffs; B buries them behind a link.
4. **Zep's incorporation-by-reference (B)** works for them with legal counsel and a stable template steward; Tortoise is a solo-operator static site — the fragility outweighs the text savings.
5. **C (generator services)** fails on content quality (generic B2C-ish text, subscription cost, weak BSL/no-training fit) — rejected on fit, not feasibility.

### 5.2 Rejected alternatives (one line each)

- **B (incorporate-by-reference):** silent-change + link-rot risk on a compliance document for a static host with no backend — rejected on immutability grounds.
- **C (iubenda/Termly/TermsFeed):** recurring cost, generic text, cannot express the BSL boundary or the no-training differentiator with the precision D5 requires — rejected on fit.
- **Copying Mem0/Zep verbatim:** imports promises never intended (Mem0 trains on Free-plan content; Zep terms predate CSA v2.1 ML clause) — rejected on accuracy (research §3.5, 4 independent practitioner sources).
