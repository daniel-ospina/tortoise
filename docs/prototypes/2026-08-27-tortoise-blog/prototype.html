Why Confidence Is a Belief, Not a Score

By Tortoise team·Aug 27, 2026·6 min read·epistemic graphs
Share 𝕏 in f wa
A belief propagates through the graph like evidence through a network.

When an agent says "I am 0.82 confident in this claim," most people read that as a score — a single number attached to a fact. In Tortoise, it is not a score. It is a belief: the output of an inference process over everything else the graph knows.

Evidence flows like water

Every edge in the graph expresses how one belief affects another. When new evidence arrives at one node, it propagates — strengthening what supports it, weakening what contradicts it. That is EP belief propagation, and it changes what confidence means.

A confidence without a structure behind it is a guess with formatting.

This is why a graph-engineered memory behaves differently from a vector store with metadata. Retrieval gives you neighbors; propagation gives you consequences.

What changes when you model it this way

  • Contradiction is first-class. A NAND edge between two claims is a fact about the world, not a cleanup task.
  • Mitigations modulate strength. A low-confidence operator can carry a mitigation that says why.
  • Confidence decays with distance. Hops dilute belief — the graph is honest about what it inferred vs. what it observed.

Beliefs you can audit

Because the confidence is a product of structure, it is explainable. Ask the graph why a claim sits at 0.82 and it can walk you back through the evidence that raised it and the counter-evidence that capped it.

# Every relationship expresses how one belief affects another
claim = graph.get("agents-should-remember-why")
for edge in graph.antecedents(claim.id):
    print(edge.operator.op_type, edge.source.confidence)

A score answers "how sure?" A belief answers "and why?" For agents that need to justify what they know, that is the difference that matters.
