# Enterprise Readiness Assessment

**Author:** TechScale Advisory (External Engagement)
**Date:** September 28, 2026
**Prepared For:** Nexus Analytics Board of Directors
**Engagement Reference:** TSA-2026-0441

---

## Executive Summary

TechScale Advisory was retained to assess Nexus Analytics' readiness to serve enterprise customers. The assessment is based on a review of Nexus's current platform capabilities, engineering team composition, sales organization, and customer support infrastructure, conducted over a four-week period in September 2026.

Overall finding: **Nexus is not currently enterprise-ready.** However, the gap is concentrated in specific capability areas that are well-understood and addressable with targeted investment.

---

## 1. Platform Uptime

**Platform uptime of 99.2%** over Q3 is below the 99.9% threshold that most enterprise SLAs require. Three enterprise pipeline deals specifically require 99.9% uptime, and two deals have asked about reliability track record. This metric alone would disqualify Nexus from most enterprise procurement processes.

---

## 2. Sales Organization

The **current sales team consists entirely of mid-market account executives** — 4 AEs with no prior enterprise sales experience. Quota attainment across the team is 62%. There are zero enterprise AEs on staff. Enterprise selling requires a fundamentally different skill set: multi-stakeholder management, procurement navigation, security review processes, and longer relationship-building cycles.

The **8 active enterprise pipeline opportunities** we documented represent real demand that the company cannot effectively convert. These opportunities have an average projected ACV of $480,000, with an average sales cycle of approximately 3.8 months. Total pipeline value is $3.84M in potential ARR — but without the sales infrastructure to close them, they remain unrealized potential.

---

## 3. Engineering Team

The **14-person engineering team has no members with enterprise security or compliance experience**. Building SSO integration, audit logging systems, and SOC 2 compliance infrastructure are well-understood technical problems, but executing them without prior experience introduces schedule risk and quality risk. New hires or extensive retraining would be required.

---
