# Board Meeting Preparation Memo

**Author:** Amara Osei, CEO
**Date:** October 7, 2026
**Distribution:** Self (preparation notes for October 15 board meeting)

---

## Board Context

Our next board meeting is October 15. I want to walk into it with a clear picture of where each director stands and what I'm walking into. These are my private notes — not polished, just honest.

---

## 1. VentureCo (Lead Investor)

VentureCo's partner, Michael, called me last week. Not a scheduled call — he reached out directly. The message was clear: "Mid-market analytics is a feature, not a company. You need to go upmarket or you're going to get eaten." He cited the competitive dynamics (the free-tier entrants, DataSight's Series B, Tableau's SMB pricing) as evidence that the mid-market window is closing.

He's pushing hard for an enterprise pivot. He pointed to the pipeline numbers — $480K ACV deals vs. $18K — and asked why we're still organized around the smaller deals.

I should note: VentureCo's incentive is growth at any cost. They need a big outcome from this investment to justify their fund returns. An enterprise pivot is the higher-variance bet — bigger potential outcome, bigger potential flameout. They're structurally incentivized to push for the high-variance path even if it increases risk. I need to recognize that their incentive structure may not fully align with the company's best interest here.

---

## 2. Board Sentiment on My Role

I've picked up signals — nothing explicit, but clear enough — that two of the five board members are watching our MRR trajectory closely. The milestone that's been discussed informally is **$200,000 MRR within six months**.

Current MRR: $142,000. At our current 4% monthly growth rate, we'd reach approximately $180,000 in six months. That's short of the $200K threshold.

I don't believe either of these two directors has made a decision. But if we miss that milestone and there isn't a compelling narrative about why and what comes next, I expect the conversation shifts from "how do we support the CEO" to "should we change the CEO."

---

## 3. StrategicCo Term Sheet

StrategicCo, a growth-stage fund that has been tracking us since Series A, delivered an informal term sheet last week: **$5 million Series A extension at flat valuation.** The terms are reasonable.

But there are two conditions:

1. The company must commit to an **enterprise pivot** — they're not interested in funding a mid-market PLG company.
2. **Sarah (CTO) must commit to staying** for at least 18 months. They did due diligence on the engineering team and identified her as a key-person risk.

The $5M would roughly double our runway and fund the transition — hiring enterprise AEs, security engineers, and the compliance work we'd need. Without that funding, an enterprise pivot means burning through our existing cash faster with no guarantee of revenue in time.

I haven't shared this term sheet with the full board yet. I want to have my own thinking clear first about whether this is the path we should take, or whether I'm being pulled in a direction by investor incentives that isn't actually right for the company.

---

## 4. Bridge Option

An existing secondary investor (not board member) has indicated willingness to provide a **$2 million bridge round** with no strategic conditions attached. This would extend our runway modestly without forcing any particular direction.

The trade-off is that a bridge round signals weakness — it's what you do when you're not ready to raise a proper round and need more time to figure things out. In the current fundraising environment, a bridge can make the next priced round harder, not easier.

---

## 5. What I Need to Decide Before the Board Meeting

I need to walk into October 15 with a point of view, not just a set of options. The board will look to me for direction. If I don't have one, they'll fill the vacuum — and that rarely goes well for the CEO.

The questions I'm wrestling with:

- Is the enterprise pivot the right move for the company, or is it the move investors want because it serves their economics?
- If we pivot, do we have the team and the cash to survive the transition period?
- If we don't pivot, can we actually hit $200K MRR in the timeline the board expects?
- What does Sarah actually need to stay? (I need to have that conversation before the board meeting.)
